## 1) Cognitive Approach to Personality

The cognitive approach says that personality is shaped by a person’s **thoughts, beliefs, interpretations, and mental processes**.  
Main idea:

**You do not react to events directly; you react to your thoughts about events.**

### Example

If exam result is average:

- one student thinks, **“I am not smart enough”** and loses motivation
- another thinks, **“I need a new strategy”** and improves performance

### Key theorists

- **Aaron Beck**
- **George Kelly**

---

## a) Cognitive Schemas

Schemas are **mental frameworks** that help people organize information and understand experiences. They act like **filters** through which new situations are interpreted. These schemas develop from past learning and experiences.

### Example

- **Positive schema**: “I can learn difficult subjects with effort.”  
    This leads to persistence.
- **Negative schema**: “I am bad at presentations.”  
    This leads to avoidance.

### Easy meaning

Schema = the way your mind is already trained to look at situations.

---

## b) Self-Concept

Self-concept means a person’s **beliefs and perceptions about themselves**, including their strengths, abilities, and limitations.  
It strongly affects:

- behaviour
- confidence
- emotions

### Easy meaning

Self-concept = “What do I think about myself?”

---

## c) Self-Talk

Self-talk is the **inner dialogue** a person has with themselves.  
It plays an important role in shaping self-concept.

### Types

**Positive self-talk**

- increases confidence
- keeps mind calm
- improves performance

**Negative self-talk**

- increases anxiety
- creates self-doubt
- reduces performance

### Example

Before viva or placement interview:

- **“I will mess up.”** → nervous, blank mind
- **“I am prepared; I will do my best.”** → better performance

### Important line

**Your inner dialogue shapes your outer personality.**

---

## d) Attributional Style

Attributional style means the way people **explain the causes of success and failure**.

### a) Optimistic attribution style

- sees failure as **temporary and changeable**
- sees success as due to **ability and effort**

Examples:

- “I did not practice enough yet.”
- “I succeeded because of my ability and effort.”

### b) Pessimistic attribution style

- sees failure as **permanent** and due to personal weakness
- sees success as due to **luck or outside help**

Examples:

- “I failed because I am dumb.”
- “I succeeded because of luck.”

---

## -> Key takeaway of Cognitive Approach

Thinking patterns influence:

- confidence
- motivation
- stress management
- leadership ability
- social skills
- career success

# Social-Cognitive Approach

## -> Meaning of Social-Cognitive Approach

The social-cognitive approach was given by **Albert Bandura**.  
It explains personality as the result of continuous interaction between:

- **personal factors** (thoughts, beliefs)
- **behaviour**
- **environment**

### Main idea

Personality is shaped not only by thinking, but also by:

- what we do
- what we observe
- the environment around us

---
## CORE PRINCIPLES OF SOCIAL_COGNITIVE APPROACH
## a) Observational Learning

Observational learning means people can learn new behaviours by **watching others**, even without direct personal experience. Role models influence behaviour and personality.

### Includes imitation of

- skills
- attitudes
- coping styles

### Example

Observing a senior who manages academics well may motivate a student to follow similar habits.

## 9) Self-Efficacy

Self-efficacy means a person’s **belief in their own ability to succeed in a specific task or situation**. It is one of the strongest predictors of performance and motivation.

### High self-efficacy

- accepts challenges
- works hard
- keeps trying
- improves with effort

### Low self-efficacy

- avoids challenges
- gives up easily
- doubts ability

### Example

- “I can learn programming with effort.” → keeps practicing and improves
- “I cannot do it.” → stops trying after difficulty

---

## 10) Reciprocal Determinism

Reciprocal determinism means **personal factors, behaviour, and environment influence each other continuously**.

### Example

A confident student:

- participates actively in class (**behaviour**)
- gets positive feedback from teacher (**environment**)
- becomes even more confident (**personal factor**)

### Easy meaning

Your thoughts affect behaviour, behaviour affects environment, and environment again affects your thoughts.

---

## 11) Difference between Cognitive and Social-Cognitive Approach

### Cognitive Approach

Focuses mainly on:

- thoughts
- beliefs
- interpretations
- mental processes

**Simple idea:** how you think shapes your personality.

### Social-Cognitive Approach

Focuses on:

- thoughts
- behaviour
- environment
- observation and role models

**Simple idea:** how you think + behave + interact with the environment shapes your personality.
