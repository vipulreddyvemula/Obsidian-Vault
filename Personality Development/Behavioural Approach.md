## Behavioural Approach to Personality — Short Notes

### 1) Meaning of behavioural approach

personality is **not something fixed that you are simply born with**. Instead, personality is a collection of **learned behaviours** that develop over time. These behaviours are shaped by:

- your **environment**
- your **experiences**
- the **rewards** you get
- the **consequences** of your actions

### 2) Main behavioural theorists

The two important theorists in this PDF are:

- **Ivan Pavlov**
- **B.F. Skinner**

---

## 3) Classical Conditioning — Ivan Pavlov

Classical conditioning is a **learning process through association**. A neutral situation becomes linked with a fear or response after a strong experience.

### Easy meaning

A person starts reacting to something because it has become associated with a previous emotional experience.

### Example 1: Fear of viva

Before conditioning, viva is just a normal situation.  
After one humiliating viva experience, the student feels fear, shame, and anxiety.  
Later, every viva creates fear automatically.  
Result:

- avoidance of viva
- blank mind
- over-preparation or withdrawal
- belief: “I am bad at viva”

### Example 2: Fear of speaking in class

Before conditioning, speaking in class is normal.  
If the student is mocked by teacher or classmates, speaking becomes associated with embarrassment and fear.  
Later, the student:

- stops raising hand
- avoids eye contact
- speaks softly or remains silent
- thinks “I will be judged”

### Generalisation

Fear may spread from one situation to many others, such as:

- group discussions
- presentations
- public speaking

---

## 4) Reconditioning

Reconditioning means reducing learned fear by **gradual exposure in a safe and supportive setting**, like mock vivas or peer practice. Repeated exposure without negative consequences weakens fear and creates new positive or neutral associations. Over time, confidence increases and avoidance reduces.

---

## 5) Operant Conditioning — B.F. Skinner

Operant conditioning explains that behaviour is shaped by its **consequences**.

- behaviour followed by rewards tends to increase
- behaviour followed by negative outcomes tends to decrease  
    Repeated behaviour gradually becomes part of personality.

### Easy meaning

**Classical conditioning = learning by association**  
**Operant conditioning = learning by consequences**

---

## 6) Core Concepts of Operant Conditioning

### a) Positive Reinforcement

**Adding something pleasant to increase behaviour**.  
Example: when a student answers in class and gets appreciation, speaking up increases. Over time, the student becomes more confident and expressive.

### b) Negative Reinforcement

**Removing something unpleasant to increase behaviour**.  
Example: when a student cleans the room and the parent stops nagging, cleaning behaviour increases. Over time, the student becomes more responsible and disciplined.

### c) Positive Punishment

**Adding something unpleasant to decrease behaviour**.  
Example: when a student talks in class and the teacher scolds them, talking decreases. Over time, the student becomes quieter and more compliant in class.

### d) Negative Punishment

**Removing something pleasant to decrease behaviour**.  
Example: when a student gets low marks and the parent takes away a video game, careless study behaviour reduces and disciplined study behaviour increases.

---

## 7) Difference between reinforcement and punishment

- **Reinforcement** increases behaviour
- **Punishment** decreases behaviour
- **Positive** means something is added
- **Negative** means something is removed

---

## 8) Key ideas of behavioural approach

- personality is learned through behaviour
- environment plays a major role
- behaviour is shaped by rewards and punishments
- what is learned can be unlearned and relearned
- small daily actions shape long-term personality
