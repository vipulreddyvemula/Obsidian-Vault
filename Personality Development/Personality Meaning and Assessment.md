### 1) Meaning of Personality

Personality means the **relatively stable and lasting pattern of thoughts, emotions, and behavior** that makes one person different from another. It shows how a person **thinks, feels, behaves, relates to others, and responds to situations**. Personality is generally:

- **consistent over time**
- influenced by **biological and environmental factors**

### 2) Classic Definitions

**Allport:** Personality is the **dynamic organization** within the individual of those psychophysical systems that determine characteristic behavior and thought.  
**Eysenck:** Personality is a **stable and enduring organization** of character, temperament, intellect, and physique.

### 3) Assessment of Personality

Personality assessment means the **systematic measurement of personality traits, patterns, and characteristics** using standard methods.


Personality is assessed:

- to understand **individual differences**
- to **predict behavior**
- to help in **diagnosis and treatment**
- to guide **educational, vocational, and clinical decisions**
## Methods of Personality Assessment

### 5) Self-Report Inventories

These are **questionnaires** in which people answer about their own thoughts, feelings, and behavior.

**Examples:**

• Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI)
• Eysenck Personality Questionnaire (EPQ)
• NEO Personality Inventory (Big Five)
• 16 Personality Factor Questionnaire (16PF)

**Merits:**

- easy to administer
- objective scoring
- useful for large groups

**Limitations:**

- social desirability bias
- faking of responses
- depends on self-awareness

### 6) Projective Techniques

These assess personality by giving **ambiguous stimuli** and analyzing the person’s interpretation.

**Examples:**

- Rorschach Inkblot Test
- Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)
- Sentence Completion Test
- Draw-A-Person Test

**Merits:**

- reveals unconscious motives and conflicts
- difficult to fake

**Limitations:**

- subjective interpretation
- time-consuming
- needs trained professionals

### 7) Behavioral Assessment

Personality is assessed by **observing actual behavior** in real or simulated situations.

**Methods:**

- naturalistic observation
- role play
- behavioral checklists

**Merit:**

- focuses on real behavior

**Limitation:**

- observer bias

### 8) Interview Method

A **face-to-face method** used to collect detailed information about personality.

**Types:**

- structured interview
- unstructured interview
- semi-structured interview

**Merits:**

- flexible
- in-depth
- allows clarification

**Limitations:**

- time-consuming
- interviewer bias

## Models of Healthy Personality

### 9) Healthy Personality

A healthy personality means a **well-functioning and balanced personality**, not just absence of mental problems.

### a) Maslow’s Self-Actualizing Personality

Maslow says a healthy person is one who has satisfied basic needs and is moving toward **self-actualization**.

**Characteristics:**

- realistic perception of self and world
- acceptance of self and others
- autonomy and independence
- creativity and spontaneity
- strong purpose in life
- deep interpersonal relationships

### b) Rogers’ Fully Functioning Person

According to Rogers, healthy personality develops through **unconditional positive regard**.

**Features:**

- openness to experience
- living in the present (Existential living)
- trust in one’s experience
- creativity and adaptability
- congruence between real self and ideal self

### c) Freud’s Mature Personality

A healthy personality is one where:

- the **ego is strong**
- the ego properly balances **id and superego**
- the **reality principle** dominates

### d) Allport’s Mature Personality

Allport talks about **psychological maturity**.  
He says a mature person has certain qualities like extension of self, warm relations, emotional security, realistic perception, and self-insight

Allport’s model is about being a **balanced, responsible, emotionally stable adult**.

Allport says psychological maturity includes:

- extension of self
- warm relations with others
- emotional security
- realistic perception
- self-insight and humor

### e) Big Five Model (OCEAN)

A healthy personality also shows balanced levels of the five major traits:

- **O – Openness** → curiosity
- **C – Conscientiousness** → responsibility
- **E – Extraversion** → sociability
- **A – Agreeableness** → empathy
- **N – Neuroticism** → low emotional instability

## Very Short Revision Points

### 15) One-line answers to remember

- **Personality** = stable pattern of thoughts, emotions, and behavior.
- **Assessment of personality** = systematic measurement of personality traits.
- **Self-report** = person answers questions about self.
- **Projective techniques** = ambiguous stimuli reveal unconscious mind.
- **Behavioral assessment** = observe actual behavior.
- **Interview** = direct face-to-face personality assessment.
- **Self-actualizer** = person moving toward highest potential.
- **OCEAN** = Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.
