### ) Psychoanalytic approach

The psychoanalytic approach was given by **Sigmund Freud**, known as the **father of psychoanalysis**. Freud said that much of human behavior is controlled by the **unconscious mind**, and that **early childhood experiences** strongly shape adult personality. Unresolved childhood conflicts may later appear as habits, fears, anxiety, or relationship problems.

### 2) Main assumptions of Freud

- Human behavior is influenced by **unconscious thoughts, emotions, and conflicts**
- **Childhood experiences** shape adult personality
- Hidden conflicts can affect present behavior
- Personality is not controlled only by conscious thinking

---

## 3) Freud’s Iceberg Model of Mind

Freud compared the mind to an **iceberg**. A small part is visible above water, but a much bigger part remains hidden below.

### a) Conscious mind

This is the part we are aware of right now.  
It includes:

- present thoughts
- current perceptions
- everyday awareness and decisions

### b) Preconscious mind

This contains information not in present awareness but which can be recalled when needed.  
It includes:

- stored memories
- knowledge
- past experiences
- Things we can recall with effort

### c) Unconscious mind

This is the deepest and largest hidden part of the mind. It strongly influences behavior.  
It includes:

- deep fears and anxieties
- repressed memories(especially traumatic ones)
- instinctual drives (like aggression or sexual urges)
- hidden or unacceptable desires

### One-line difference

- **Conscious** = what you know now
- **Preconscious** = what you can recall
- **Unconscious** = what is hidden but still affects you

## 4) Structure of Personality: Id, Ego, Superego

Freud explained personality through three parts: **Id, Ego, and Superego**.

### a) Id

- instinct-driven part
- wants immediate satisfaction
- works on **pleasure principle**
- childlike part of personality

Example: “I want it right now.”

### b) Ego

- rational and practical part
- balances the id with reality
- works on **reality principle**
- acts like a manager

Example: “Let us find a practical way.”

### c) Superego

- moral part of personality
- represents values, conscience, right and wrong
- works on **morality principle**
- acts like a parent or moral judge

Example: “Good people should not think like that.”

### Easy memory trick

- **Id** = desire
- **Ego** = decision
- **Superego** = duty

## 5) Defence Mechanisms

Defence mechanisms are **unconscious mental processes** used to protect a person from anxiety, stress, or uncomfortable feelings.

### Important defence mechanisms

**1. Rationalization**  
Giving logical reasons to justify failure or wrong behavior.  
Example: “I failed because the paper was unfair.”

**2. Denial**  
Refusing to accept reality.  
Example: Saying “I am not stressed” even when deadlines are increasing.

**3. Projection**  
Attributing your own feelings to others.  
Example: “The professor dislikes me,” when actually you dislike the professor or subject.

**4. Displacement**  
Shifting emotion from the real source to a safer target.  
Example: Getting scolded by a professor and then shouting at a friend.

**5. Regression**  
Returning to immature behavior during stress.  
Example: Crying after poor exam performance.

**6. Sublimation**  
Channeling emotions into useful or productive activity.  
	Example: Using anger to work out or play sports. This is a **healthy defence mechanism**.

**7. Reaction Formation**  
Behaving opposite to true feelings.  
Example: Acting overly confident while actually feeling anxious.

### Best ones to memorize first

- Rationalization
- Denial
- Projection
- Displacement
- Sublimation

## 6) Neo-psychoanalytic approach

Neo-psychoanalytic thinkers accepted that **early childhood experiences** and the **unconscious mind** matter, but they changed Freud’s theory by giving more importance to **social, cultural, and interpersonal factors**. They also emphasized **conscious thought, relationships, and personal growth**.

### Main idea

Freud focused more on **inner biological drives**, while neo-psychoanalytic thinkers focused more on:

- social environment
- relationships
- conscious coping
- life experiences
- personal growth

### Names shown in the note

- Alfred Adler
- Erik Erikson
- Carl Jung
- Karen Horney

---

## 7) Freud vs Neo-psychoanalytic approach

**Freud**

- unconscious mind is central
- childhood experiences shape personality
- stronger focus on instincts and biological drives

**Neo-psychoanalytic**

- accepts unconscious and childhood importance
- adds social, cultural, and interpersonal influences
- gives more role to conscious thought and relationships
