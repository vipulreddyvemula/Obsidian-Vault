## Humanistic and Traits Approach — Short Notes

### 1) Humanistic approach to personality

The humanistic approach says people are **inherently good**, have **free will**, and are capable of **growth and self-development**. It focuses on the **positive side of human nature**, personal meaning, conscious choices, and mainly the **present and future** rather than the past.

### 2) Main theorists of humanistic approach

- **Abraham Maslow**
- **Carl Rogers**

---

## Maslow

### 3) Maslow’s hierarchy of needs

Maslow said human motivation is based on a **hierarchy of needs**. Lower needs must be satisfied before higher ones.

Order of needs:

- **Physiological needs** — food, water, sleep
- **Safety needs** — protection, security
- **Love and belongingness** — friendship, family, affection
- **Esteem needs** — respect, recognition, confidence
- **Self-actualization** — becoming the **best version of oneself**

### 4) Self-actualization

Self-actualization is the highest level in Maslow’s theory. It means realizing one’s full potential and becoming what one is capable of becoming. A student can focus on growth and achievement only after basic needs like food and safety are reasonably satisfied.

### 5) Characteristics of self-actualizers / healthy personality

Useful points to remember:

- realistic perception of self and world
- acceptance of self and others
- autonomy and independence
- creativity and spontaneity
- strong purpose in life
- deep interpersonal relationships

---

## Rogers

### 6) Rogers’ self-concept

Carl Rogers said personality depends a lot on **self-concept**, which means how a person sees and understands themselves.

### 7) Unconditional positive regard

Rogers believed people grow best when they receive **unconditional positive regard**, meaning love, acceptance, and support **without judgment or conditions**.  
If approval depends only on performance, it creates pressure, self-doubt, and lower self-esteem.

### 8) Real self and ideal self

- **Real self** = who we actually are
- **Ideal self** = who we want to become

### 9) Congruence and incongruence

- **Congruence** = real self and ideal self are close; this leads to psychological well-being
- **Incongruence** = large gap between real self and ideal self; this leads to distress and low self-esteem

---

## Traits approach

### 10) Meaning of traits approach

The traits approach sees personality as a collection of **stable characteristics**, called **traits**, that influence how a person thinks, feels, and behaves. Everyone has the same basic traits, but in **different degrees**, and these differences create different personalities. This approach mainly **describes** personality rather than explaining how it develops.

### 11) Main theorists of traits approach

- **Gordon Allport**
- **Raymond Cattell**
- **Hans Eysenck**

---

## Allport

### 12) Allport’s trait theory

Allport divided traits into **three levels**:

**a) Cardinal traits**  
Rare dominant traits that shape a person’s whole life and identity.  
Example: lifelong commitment to truth or power.

**b) Central traits**  
General traits seen in most situations and commonly used to describe a person.  
Examples: honest, friendly, kind, intelligent, responsible, confident.

**c) Secondary traits**  
Specific preferences or reactions seen only in certain situations.  
Examples: nervous before exams, liking spicy food, particular clothing choices.

---

## Cattell

### 13) Cattell’s trait theory

Cattell said personality is made up of **16 basic traits**. These traits lie on a **scale**, so a person is not simply one type or another. For example, someone may be neither fully shy nor fully outgoing, but somewhere in between.

---

## Eysenck

### 14) Eysenck’s PEN model

Eysenck said personality can be understood through **three major dimensions**, called **PEN**:

- **P – Psychoticism**: aggressiveness, impulsivity, tough-mindedness
- **E – Extraversion**: sociability, energy, talkativeness
- **N – Neuroticism**: emotional instability, anxiety, stress proneness

People low in extraversion are more introverted, and people low in neuroticism are calmer and emotionally stable.

---

## 15) Difference between humanistic and traits approach

**Humanistic approach**

- focuses on growth, free will, self-concept, and personal potential
- says people are naturally good
- cares about becoming the best version of oneself

**Traits approach**

- focuses on stable characteristics
- describes personality in terms of measurable traits
- explains personality differences by different levels of traits

---

## 16) Very short revision points

- **Humanistic approach** = people are naturally good and capable of growth.
- **Maslow** = hierarchy of needs and self-actualization.
- **Rogers** = self-concept, unconditional positive regard, real self, ideal self.
- **Congruence** = real self and ideal self are similar.
- **Traits approach** = personality is a set of stable traits.
- **Allport** = cardinal, central, secondary traits.
- **Cattell** = 16 basic traits.
- **Eysenck** = PEN model.
