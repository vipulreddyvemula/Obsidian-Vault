## 2. Ambiguity in Language

**Lexical ambiguity:** A word has multiple meanings.

Example: _Bat_ = sports equipment or an animal.

**Syntactic ambiguity:** A sentence has multiple interpretations because of its grammatical structure.

Example: "I saw the man with the telescope."

- Interpretation 1: I used a telescope to see the man.
- Interpretation 2: The man had a telescope.

|Lexical ambiguity|Syntactic ambiguity|
|---|---|
|Multiple meanings of a word|Multiple interpretations of sentence structure|
|Example: _Bank_|Example: "I saw the man with the telescope."|

**Contextual disambiguation:** Using surrounding information to identify the intended meaning.

**World knowledge:** Knowledge about real-world objects and situations that helps us interpret language.

Example: "The trophy wouldn't fit in the suitcase because it was too small." Here, the suitcase was too small.

**Remember:** Understanding language requires more than knowing dictionary definitions. Sept16_17_notes_Cognitive_Scien…

## 3. Sapir–Whorf Hypothesis

Also known as **linguistic relativity**, associated with Edward Sapir and Benjamin Lee Whorf.

**Core idea:** The structure and habitual use of language can influence how speakers think about and understand the world.

### Three levels of claims

|Claim|Meaning|Strength|
|---|---|---|
|Linguistic determinism|Language determines thought|Strong|
|Linguistic relativity|Language influences thought|Moderate|
|Language reflects thought|Language reflects environmental and cultural interests|Weakest|

**Important distinction:**

- **Determines:** Language limits what you can think.
- **Influences:** Language affects habitual thinking without completely determining it.
- **Reflects:** Language develops in ways that reflect what matters to people and their culture.

### "No word ≠ No thought"

A person can experience or think about something without having a specific word for it.

This challenges the strong claim that language determines everything we can think.

**Quiz tip:** Language determining thought = linguistic determinism; language influencing thought = linguistic relativity. Sept16_17_notes_Cognitive_Scien…

## 4. Five Properties of Language

|Property|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Communicative|Expresses thoughts, feelings, and intentions|Telling someone your plans|
|Arbitrary|Word–meaning relationships are generally conventional|The same object has different names in different languages|
|Structured|Follows grammatical rules|Subject + Verb + Object|
|Generative|A finite set of words and rules can produce many sentences|Creating new sentences|
|Dynamic|Changes over time|New words such as _selfie_|

### Language vs. Speech

**Language is not the same as speech.**

- Auditory modality: Spoken language.
- Visual modality: Written language and sign language.

Speech is one way of expressing language; language does not necessarily require sound. Sept16_17_notes_Cognitive_Scien…

## 5. Phonemes vs. Morphemes

**Phoneme:** The smallest sound unit that can distinguish one word from another. It does not itself carry meaning.

Examples: _bat → pat_, _fan → van_.

**Morpheme:** The smallest unit of meaning in a language.

Examples:

- `books` = `book + s`
- `unhappy` = `un + happy`
- `redo` = `re + do`
- `teacher` = `teach + er`

|Phoneme|Morpheme|
|---|---|
|Sound unit|Meaningful unit|
|Does not itself carry meaning|Carries meaning or a grammatical function|
|Example: /b/ in _bat_|Example: `un-` in _unhappy_|

**This distinction is especially important for MCQs.** Sept16_17_notes_Cognitive_Scien…

## 6. Linguistic Hierarchy

Memorize this sequence:

**Phonemes → Morphemes → Words → Phrases → Sentences → Meaning**

- Phonemes: Sound units.
- Morphemes: Meaningful units.
- Words: Linguistic units formed from morphemes.
- Phrases: Groups of words functioning together.
- Sentences: Structured combinations of words and phrases.
- Meaning: Interpretation of the linguistic expression.

## 7. Grammar — The Four Pillars

**Grammar:** The system of rules governing how linguistic elements are organized.

|Component|Studies|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**Phonology**|Sound patterns|/s/ and /z/|
|**Morphology**|Word structure|`walk + ed = walked`|
|**Syntax**|Arrangement of words|Subject + Verb + Object|
|**Semantics**|Meaning|Interpreting a sentence|

### Memory trick

**Sounds → Words → Sentences → Meaning**

- Phonology = sounds.
- Morphology = word formation.
- Syntax = sentence structure.
- Semantics = meaning.

These four components are central to the grammar section of your slides. Sept16_17_notes_Cognitive_Scien…

## 8. Natural Language Processing (NLP)

**NLP:** Computational processing of human language.

An NLP system must deal with different linguistic levels and may need to use context to interpret meaning.

### Simplified NLP pipeline

**Input → Phonology → Morphology → Syntax → Semantics → Meaning**

Input may be speech, text, or sign.

### Speech recognition pipeline

**Speech signal → Acoustic information → Phonemes → Words → Sentence**

The system must map sound information to linguistic representations. Sept16_17_notes_Cognitive_Scien…

- **Development:** How does the child's language ability change with age?
- **Network models:** How is _dog_ connected to _animal_, _pet_, and _puppy_?
- **Philosophy:** What does it mean for the child to understand the concept?
